# Negs 0.18.0

The Windows app gains the Mac's interface, library, film strip and grid,
film and paper tray, Compare, batch export and Open in Lightroom. Every
platform now falls back to LibRaw for RAW files rawler can't read
(including Nikon HE and HE★), and 17 Fujifilm films prepared from
Fujifilm's datasheets join Mac, Windows and Web behind Settings.

## Everywhere

- **More RAW cameras (LibRaw fallback).** RAW files rawler can't decode—
  including Nikon High Efficiency (HE and HE★) NEFs—now open via LibRaw
  on Windows, Linux and Mac.
- **17 Fujifilm films from datasheets.** Five Eterna cine negatives, five
  still negatives (Pro 160S, Pro 160C, Pro 800Z, Superia 1600 and True
  Definition 400) and seven slides (Velvia 100F, Provia 400F, Provia
  400X, 64T Type II and Sensia 100/200/400), available on Mac, Windows
  and Web when **Settings › Show Negs-built films** is turned on (off by
  default).

## Windows

The Windows app now looks and works like the Mac's: its themes and
inspector, the print with its caption and label, the film and paper tray,
a library with ratings and edits beside your photographs, the film strip
and grid, and Compare.

- **The Mac's look.** View › Theme has the Mac's six themes: Plain, Plain
  dark, Darkroom, Contact sheet, Light table and Daylight. New users
  start on Plain or Plain dark to match Windows' light or dark mode.
- **A quieter window.** One band at the top holds the menus (File, Edit,
  Photo, View, Help), the photograph's name (or the photograph selected
  in the grid) and Grid · Photo · Compare. The toolbar is gone: its
  commands are in the menus, with Ctrl shortcuts (Crop is Ctrl+K,
  Settings Ctrl+,). Ctrl+O and dropping a folder open the folder
  directly, as on the Mac. Snap layouts, Alt, F10 and the underlined
  letters work as before.
- **A status line in words** under the photograph: its size, how long it
  took to develop, notes from the library, or the export under way with
  Cancel. Original ⌄ shows the original while you hold it, as a split
  line or side by side, and for a RAW+JPEG pair the camera's JPEG. Fit
  and 100% zoom.
- **Messages under the title bar.** Errors and questions hang from the
  top of the window instead of in dialog boxes. A photograph that can't
  be developed says so where it would be, with Try Again.
- **The Mac's inspector.** Prep, Lab and Output are tabs, each with a
  count of its edits; click the count to see or reset them. A changed
  value is bold and underlined in pencil, ↺ resets one value, and rarely
  used controls fold into "N more" lines (View › Show All Controls shows
  them all). Output ends with Export Photograph….
- **Presets as tiles**, each showing your photograph developed through
  it, with its film and paper. Click to apply, drag to sort, drop preset
  files to import them.
- **Settings in its own window** (Ctrl+,), with the Mac's tabs:
  Photographs, Speed, Upkeep and Look. Photographs › Fotufilm films
  lists all of Fotufilm's films, only those made from datasheets, or
  none.
- **The photograph as a print.** The camera's settings sit above it and
  "Film ⌄ printed on Paper ⌄" under it. The lock on "printed on" keeps
  the paper when you change the film, replacing the "Keep paper when
  changing film" setting. Fitted, the photograph is resampled to exactly
  the pixels it fills, so fine detail stays sharp.
- **The film and paper tray.** Click the film or the paper under the
  photograph to see every stock as your photograph developed with it,
  your favourites first. Click a tile to try it, ← → to step; Return or
  Done keeps it as one step to undo, Esc goes back. The film tray has
  Search. Film and Paper are no longer in the inspector.
- **Viewer sheets.** View › Viewer Sheet (and Settings › Look › Viewer ›
  Sheet) puts the photograph on one of seven sheets, from white to black,
  in place of the custom background colour. A border that would vanish
  into the sheet sits on a light table's glow.
- **Image Only and upside down.** F shows the photograph alone; holding
  T turns it upside down. Esc leaves Image Only.
- **Cropping in the Mac's style.** Trim marks at the print's corners
  start a crop, with the aspect ratios, Landscape and Portrait, the angle
  and the size in a panel and a dial to straighten. Return applies, Esc
  cancels.
- **Flip.** "Flip left to right" in the crop panel (and View › Flip
  Horizontal) mirrors the photograph and its exports, as a negative
  scanned from its base side needs.
- **A library in every folder.** Ratings, flags and edits are saved in
  small files beside your photographs, the same files the Mac app reads
  and writes. The Photo menu rates, flags, copies and pastes edits,
  duplicates, resets, filters and moves photographs to the Recycle Bin,
  and Undo covers all of it. Deleting a photograph keeps any duplicates
  you didn't select. Exports carry the rating. A RAW and its camera JPEG
  show as one photograph. Settings › Photographs can turn off either.
- **The film strip and the grid.** The strip shows each photograph at
  its own shape, with hand-drawn stars, picks, rejects and a dot for
  edits. G fades the folder in as a contact sheet over the window: filter
  it, select with click, Ctrl, Shift, a drag or the arrow keys, change
  the size with Ctrl and the wheel, and go back with E, G, Return, Esc or
  a double click.
- **Thumbnails as you developed them**, cropped and bordered, made in the
  background with the ones in view first, keeping their shapes as a large
  folder opens. A photograph you come back to opens on its last develop
  while it develops again. Negs no longer writes `Thumbs.db` files into
  network folders.
- **Compare.** Select two photographs and press C to see them side by
  side, or in one frame with a line you drag (S switches). Click either
  to edit it; ← → step through the roll. The status line says how they
  differ.
- **Export the grid's selection.** With several photographs selected,
  File › Export exports each with its own edits to a folder, named as you
  choose. The status line shows how far it has come; Show lists anything
  that failed, with Retry Failed. An export cut short by quitting is
  offered again at the next launch.
- **Open in Lightroom.** With Lightroom or Lightroom Classic installed,
  File, the Output tab and the right-click menus send the photographs to
  it as full-size 16-bit TIFFs. Settings › Upkeep › Lightroom shows how
  much is kept for it, with a button to delete it.
- **Your negative.** The film tray starts with "Your negative", which
  prints a scan of a colour negative: it finds the film base, balances
  and meters the print and crops to the picture. Prep gains Film base,
  Flat field and Balance, and Use for whole roll prints every photograph
  in view as frames of the same roll.
- **B&W colour filters** in Lab › Camera, with how many stops each takes.
- **Enlarger › Shifts move** links the magenta and yellow shifts:
  separately, together or opposite.
- **Faster opening and print edits.** A RAW's brightness match is solved
  before anything develops, so there is no darker or lighter first frame,
  and a photograph you come back to skips it. Discrete GPUs also keep the
  developed film side between renders so print and scan adjustments are
  quicker.
- Image Size › Longest side falls back to the crop's own longest side
  when the remembered value doesn't fit.
- Screen readers name every control, the tray's tiles and stars too.
- The window reopens at the size it was left at, at 125 % and 150 %
  display scaling too.
