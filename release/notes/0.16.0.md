# Negs 0.16.0

The Mac app prints your own scanned colour negatives, compares two
photographs side by side, comes in five themes, pairs a RAW with its
camera JPEG and sends photographs to Lightroom. Black-and-white films take
colour filters on the Mac and the web, and every app gains colour noise
reduction in the scanner.

## Everywhere

- **Colour noise reduction.** Scanner › Color noise reduction (0 to 100)
  takes the colour out of the grain and keeps its texture, as lab scanner
  software does. Each pixel keeps its brightness, and the reach follows the
  film format. At 0, the default, renders are as before.

## Mac

- **Your negative.** Photograph or scan a real colour negative and Negs
  prints it. Choose Your negative in the film tray: its first group, Scan,
  is where the tray opens when the photograph looks like a negative. Negs
  reads it as the film you pick, finds the film base in the clear film
  round the picture (or Pick from rebate), and balances the print on the
  neutral axis, Auto as a minilab's printer would, or Pick neutral on
  something grey. Prep holds the negative's controls: the backlight, the
  film base, the balance, a light frame for your light's falloff, and Crop
  to picture, levelled by the film's edges. Use for whole roll prints every
  photograph in view as a frame of one roll, sharing its filtration, each
  frame with its own base and exposure; scans that don't look like
  negatives are skipped. Flip sits beside rotate in cropping.
- **Compare.** Select two photographs and press C, or right-click, or
  choose Compare in the title bar's Grid · Photo · Compare, to see them
  side by side (stacked when that gives them more room) or under a slider;
  S switches between the two. The panel edits one of them; click the other
  to edit it instead. ← and → step the second through the folder, zoom and
  pan move both, and what differs is underlined under each and named in
  the status line.
- **Original ⌄.** The Original word picks how you see the photograph
  before Negs: hold it, a split line you drag, or side by side. A "Loading
  original" tag shows while a large original decodes.
- **Themes.** Settings › Look has five themes. Darkroom is the dark one
  you know, and stays the default. Contact sheet is ivory paper with a
  black film strip and a red pencil. Light table is white with a yellow
  highlighter. Daylight is a mid grey, quiet for long sessions. Plain is
  black on light grey in the system font, with blue marks, the easiest to
  read. Panels sit a tone darker than the sheet, so the photograph's
  surround never merges with them. The sheet behind the photograph now
  defaults to the theme's own; the fixed sheets are still there, and their
  greys are fairer for judging a photograph. The Viewer setting moved from
  Photographs to Look, and View › Theme switches too. A white mat on a
  light sheet gets a soft shade around it, as a black border gets a light
  on the dark one.
- **RAW + JPEG.** A RAW and the camera's JPEG with the same name
  (IMG_0412.CR3 and IMG_0412.JPG) are now one photo in the strip and grid,
  marked +JPG. The RAW is the one that develops. Deleting the photo moves
  both files to the Trash. Original ⌄ can show the camera's JPEG instead
  of the RAW, so you can hold, split or compare against the camera's own
  film simulation. A JPEG you've already rated or edited stays a photo of
  its own. To list every JPEG as before, tick Settings › Photographs ›
  Show camera JPEGs separately.
- **Open in Lightroom.** Right-click photographs in the grid or the film
  strip, or choose File › Open in Lightroom (⌥⌘E), to send them to
  Lightroom as full-size 16-bit TIFFs in your export colour space, with
  their EXIF and rating. Lightroom asks once to add them all. Negs keeps
  its copies only until Lightroom has added them, or a week at most;
  Settings › Upkeep can delete them sooner. Shown only when Lightroom is
  installed. With Lightroom Classic, Open in Lightroom Classic puts the
  TIFFs in Pictures › Negs for Lightroom, which Negs never empties by
  itself, and opens Classic's Import window. The right-click menu also has
  Export…, and a batch export started from the grid shows its progress
  there.
- **Colour filters for black-and-white film.** Lab › Camera › Color Filter
  puts a yellow-green, green, yellow, orange or red glass (Hoya X1, X0, Y2,
  YA3, R1) in front of the lens, and says how many stops it takes on the
  film you're using; Tri-X's match Kodak's datasheet. Shown for
  black-and-white films, or with a warning while a filter is left on a
  colour film.
- **Edited or unedited.** The filter menu's Edited Only is now an Edits
  section with Edited and Unedited. A filter that leaves out the grid's
  current photograph no longer keeps it shown.
- **G hides the grid.** In the grid, G now goes back to the current
  photograph, as E does.
- **The strip's arrow** points down and hides the film strip; from the
  status line, up brings it back.
- **Panasonic DC-S1M2** RAWs no longer show green, crushed shadows: Apple's
  decoder develops them wrongly, so Negs decodes them itself, as the web
  app does.
- A click in the film strip opens the photograph without centring it, and
  the grid opens and folds without flashes at the end.
- Rotated photographs' film and paper tiles in the tray and presets are
  turned as the photograph is.
- A photograph whose brightness match failed to decode no longer holds its
  frame forever, and Save no longer waits 30 s after a drag or a cancelled
  crop.
- If the engine stops, Negs says so instead of quitting.
- A folder Negs can't write to no longer shows edits that weren't saved.
  Undoing every edit after changing the new photographs' film keeps the
  photograph's film, and Paste Edits from an unedited photograph uses
  today's default film.
