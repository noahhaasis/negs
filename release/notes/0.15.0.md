# Negs 0.15.0

The Mac app is redrawn: dark ink, Newsreader type, and the film and paper as
a label under the photograph. It now keeps a library, with ratings, picks, a
grid, edits saved per photograph and batch export, and shows every
photograph developed. Fuji X-Trans RAWs decode sharp on Windows, Linux and
the web, and the web app speaks Chinese.

## Everywhere

- **Fuji X-Trans RAWs decode sharp.** They were demosaiced with a bilinear
  average that left them as if blurred by 1 to 1.5 px. Negs now uses a
  Markesteijn pass, which matches LibRaw's within about 1% at every spatial
  frequency on the X-T1 and X-E2 samples. It costs time: a 16 MP X-T1 frame
  decodes in 0.5 s instead of 0.14 s. This is every X-Trans file on Windows,
  Linux and the web, and on the Mac whenever Apple RAW declines one (X-T1,
  X-E2). Bayer and every other sensor decode as before.
- **Film and paper catalogue.** The catalogue says which films are Fotufilm
  ports and which of those are modelled from sample photographs rather than
  datasheets.
- **Faster edits to the print.** Changing the print, the scan or the post
  controls reuses the film side kept on the GPU, about 3.5 times faster.
  Exports are unchanged.

## Mac

- **A new look.** The Mac app is redrawn in dark ink, with its names and
  numbers set in Newsreader. The toolbar is gone: a slim title bar shows the
  file's name, and no control in the app uses the system blue.
- **The film and paper as a label.** Under the photograph, in large type,
  the film and the paper it's printed on. Click either to open a tray of
  stocks along the bottom of the window: click a stock to try it, step
  through them with ← and →, and keep one with Return or Done.
- **A library.** Each photograph keeps its rating, flag and edits in a
  `.negs` file beside it. Filter the folder, select photographs, copy and
  paste edits (⌘C, ⌘V), duplicate, delete to the Trash with undo, and export
  a batch with its ratings in the files.
- **The strip and the grid.** The strip keeps each photograph's
  proportions. The grid grows out of it (G, or a double-click on the
  photograph) and folds back into it, in packed rows under a sentence that
  says what it shows. Click selects, double-click opens, pinch resizes.
  Picks are boxed in grease pencil, rejects crossed out and ratings starred
  with quick asterisks, each by hand and a little differently on every
  photograph; edited photographs have a dot. In the grid, the current
  photograph and each one selected are boxed again in yellow.
- **Every thumbnail developed.** The strip and the grid show each
  photograph as Negs develops it: edited ones with their edits, unedited
  ones with a default film and paper you choose in Settings. The nearest to
  your view come first, and a photograph opens on its developed thumbnail.
  A thousand photographs take about four minutes. The cache holds up to
  1 GB and Settings can empty it. After an update, the last version's
  thumbnails stand in until the new ones are ready.
- **Quicker opening.** A photograph opens on a quick frame at its matched
  exposure, then the settled one.
- **Low Power Mode.** The grid develops only the thumbnails on screen, one
  at a time, and the rest of the folder once Low Power Mode is off.
- **The inspector.** Prep, Lab and Output as tabs, their settings as
  modules that open and close, each with a dot when it's been edited. A
  tab's count opens a list of its edits, each with a reset, and *Reset all*.
- **Enlarger › Shifts move.** Link the magenta and yellow shifts so that
  they move Together, in Opposite ways, or Separately. Linked sliders reset
  together.
- **Crop with trim marks.** ⌘K or the trim marks round the photograph:
  drag the corners or edges, straighten with the dial under the photograph,
  and turn it a quarter with the arrows at the dial's ends (⌘R, ⌥⌘R). The
  aspect ratios, orientation and size are in the panel.
- **Image Only.** F hides the strip, the status line and the panel, and
  leaves the photograph with its caption and the film and paper under it.
  F or Esc brings back what was showing.
- **Upside down.** Hold T to see the photograph upside down and judge its
  composition alone; let go to turn it back. Nothing about the photograph
  changes.
- **The sheet behind the photograph.** Settings › Viewer › Sheet offers
  Darkroom (the default), Black, Charcoal, 22% grey, Middle grey, Light grey
  and Paper, with the caption, label and marks inked to suit. A film border
  or dark mat that would vanish into the sheet gets a soft light table
  under it.
- **Fotufilm films.** Settings › Photographs › Films can show All of the
  Fotufilm films, only those From datasheets, or None. The film you are
  using stays listed.
- **A sharper fit.** The fitted photograph is resampled to the screen's
  pixels with Lanczos in linear light.
- **Presets as tiles.** Click a tile to apply it, drag to sort, drop files
  on the pane to import them.
- **Settings in three tabs.** Photographs (what a new photograph starts
  with), Speed and Upkeep, each a short page in the inspector's style, so
  the window fits a small screen. A slide film's paper is struck through.
- **Questions in the window.** Alerts are drawn in the app's own style,
  under the title bar, with the Return button marked ↩. The status line
  says in words what the app is doing, and the export's progress is drawn
  in ink.
- **VoiceOver.** The marks, modules, crop controls and alerts are labelled,
  and the grid reads in its own order.
- Pasting the edits of an unedited photograph no longer marks edits.
- The film strip holds still entering and leaving full screen, and there is
  no grey bar across the top in full screen.
- Clicking the paper's name in the film tray shows the papers; before, a
  click on a tile there could print the photograph on a film.

## Windows

- The save dialog keeps the JPEG quality and size you type. Before, they
  were read after the dialog closed and the typed quality never reached the
  encoder.

## Linux

- Settings opens at its full height, not one row.
- The open film and paper previews keep their height on the AppImage's
  GTK 4.16, and a grip under them resizes them (double-click resets it).

## Web

- **Chinese.** [getnegs.com/try](https://getnegs.com/try/) has a zh-CN
  interface, picked from your browser's language or in Settings ›
  Language.
- Settings can hide the Fotufilm films, or those not from datasheets.
- 40 MP X-Trans RAWs open on phones, demosaiced more simply for the reduced
  copy.
