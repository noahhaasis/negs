# Negs (unreleased)

## Mac

- The photo keeps its zoom and position when you change the film or paper.

## Windows

- **The Mac's look.** The window is drawn in the Mac app's themes, chosen
  in View › Theme: Darkroom (the default), Contact sheet, Light table,
  Daylight, Plain and Plain dark. Only Plain follows Windows' light or
  dark mode.
- **A quieter title bar.** One band holds File, Edit, Photo, View and
  Help, the photograph's name in Newsreader, and minimise, maximise and
  close in the theme's colours. Snap layouts still open from the
  maximise button, and Alt, F10 and the underlined letters work as
  before.
- **No toolbar.** Open, Export, the panel and the photograph's tools
  (fit, 100 %, rotate, crop, before and after, the film strip) are in
  the menus, with Ctrl shortcuts. Crop is Ctrl+K; Settings is
  Ctrl+,; Reset Edits is in the new Photo menu.
- **A status line in words** under the photograph: its size and how long
  it took to develop, or the export under way with Cancel. Original ⌄
  shows the original while you hold it, or from its menu, and Fit and
  100% zoom.
- **Messages under the title bar.** Errors and questions hang from the
  top of the window over a dimmed view, with the same words and buttons
  as before. Enter answers, Esc cancels, Tab moves between the buttons.
- With nothing open, the window shows where the print will go, "Open a
  photograph" and your recent photographs. Dragging a file over it says
  "drop to open it".
- A photograph that couldn't be developed now says so where it would be,
  with the reason and Try Again, instead of an error box. "Developing"
  and "Updating" show as a small tag at the top of the photograph.
- **The Mac's inspector.** The panel reads like the Mac's. Adjustments ·
  Presets and Histogram · Waveform are words, with Reset on the right.
  Prep, Lab and Output are tabs in Newsreader, each with a count of its
  edits; click the count to see them or reset them. Sliders have a fine
  track and a ring for a knob, ↺ resets one value, and a changed value
  turns bold and underlined in pencil. Rarely used controls fold into
  "N more" lines (View › Show All Controls shows them all), and Output
  ends with the export's size and format above Export Photograph….
  Keys, Tab focus and screen readers work as before.
- **Presets as tiles.** The Presets panel shows each preset as your
  photograph developed through it, with its name, film and paper, two to
  a row; the one you applied last is ringed. Click to apply, drag to
  sort, drop preset files to import them. Each tile's menu has Rename,
  Export…, Show in Folder and Delete…, and the arrow keys, Enter, F2 and
  Del work on the tile with the focus.
- **Settings in its own window.** File › Settings (Ctrl+,) opens a window
  in the app's look with the Mac's tabs: Photographs, Speed, Upkeep and
  Look. Look shows the six themes as small pictures of the window.
  Changes apply at once; Esc closes it.
- Settings › Photographs › Fotufilm films can list all of Fotufilm's
  films, only those made from datasheets, or none. A film already in use
  stays listed.
- **The photograph as a print.** It sits on the page as on the Mac: the
  camera, ISO, shutter, aperture and focal length above it in italic with
  the file name on the right, and "Film ⌄ printed on Paper ⌄" under it.
  Click the film or the paper to choose another; the lock on "printed
  on" keeps the paper when you change the film (this replaces the "Keep
  paper when changing film" setting). Fitted, the photograph is
  resampled to exactly the pixels it fills, so fine detail stays sharp.
- **Viewer sheets.** Settings › Look › Viewer › Sheet puts the photograph
  on the theme's own colour or one of seven sheets, from white to black;
  the caption and label change their ink to suit. It replaces the custom
  background colour. A print whose border would vanish into the sheet
  sits on a light table's soft glow.
- **Image Only and upside down.** F shows the photograph alone on its
  sheet; holding T shows it upside down, for judging the composition. Esc
  leaves Image Only first.
- **Cropping in the Mac's style.** Trim marks at the print's corners
  start a crop. The panel lists the aspect ratios with their film and
  print formats, Landscape and Portrait, the angle and the size, with a
  yellow Done; Return applies and Esc cancels. Straighten with the dial
  under the photograph; its arrows turn it a quarter.
- **The film and paper tray.** Clicking the film or the paper under the
  photograph opens a tray along the bottom of the window, in place of
  the film strip: every stock as
  your photograph developed with it, in groups, your favourites first.
  Click a tile to try it, as often as you like; ← → step through them.
  Return or Done keeps it, as one step to undo, and Esc goes back to the
  stock you started from. Clicking elsewhere, or any other edit or
  command, keeps it too. The film tray has Search; the paper tray has the
  paper lock's switch. While a stock develops its tile stands in, turned
  like the photograph. With the tiles off in Settings, the names open a
  menu instead. A group's name in the tray's foot glides the row to it,
  and screen readers name every tile, star and switch in it.
- The film and paper are chosen only under the photograph now, as on the
  Mac: Film and Paper above Prep · Lab · Output are gone, and the
  development times moved to the Lab's Film and Print sections.
- The film, the paper and the paper lock under the photograph explain
  themselves when you rest the pointer on them.
- The window reopens at exactly the size it was left at, at 125 % and
  150 % display scaling too.
- **The film strip as on the Mac.** Each frame sits on black film at its
  photograph's own shape, the open one whole with a pencil line under it
  and the rest dimmed. Ratings show as hand-drawn stars, picks in a
  pencilled box, rejects crossed out, with a soft shadow behind them, and
  edited photographs carry a dot. The tongue at the strip's right end
  hides and shows it, its slanted edge smooth. The strip and the status
  line are as tall as the tray, so the photograph no longer moves when
  the tray opens.
- **The grid.** G lays the folder out as a contact sheet over the window,
  unfolding out of the strip: rows of photographs at their own shapes,
  "Showing all ⌄ 34 photographs" above them (click the words to filter,
  × to clear) and the count and a Size slider below. Click, Ctrl, Shift,
  a drag across the gaps and the arrow keys select; ratings and flags
  apply to the selection. Ctrl+plus and minus, or Ctrl and the wheel,
  change the size. E, Return, Esc or a double click go back to the
  photograph. The wheel glides, and a thin scroll bar shows at the right
  edge while it scrolls; drag it, or click above or below it to page.
- **Thumbnails as you developed them.** The strip and the grid show each
  photograph with its edits, developed in the background and kept in
  Negs' own cache, the ones in view first (only those while energy saver
  is on), cropped, turned and inside their borders. The photograph you
  leave shows its edits at once, made from what was on screen. Negs no
  longer asks Windows for thumbnails, which wrote `Thumbs.db` files into
  network folders.
- Settings › Photographs has the Mac's switches for showing camera JPEGs
  beside their RAWs ("Show camera JPEGs separately") and for "Save
  ratings and edits next to photos". Both keep today's behaviour unless
  you change them.
- A photograph you come back to opens on its last develop, sharper than
  the camera's preview, while it develops again, even after an update.
- While cropping, the status line says the aspect and the size of the
  crop.
- **Compare.** Select two photographs in the strip or grid and press C
  (View › Compare) to see them side by side, or in one frame with a line
  you drag (S switches). Right-click offers "Compare 3 and 7", "Compare
  with 3" or "Compare with Original". Click the other photograph to edit
  it; ← → step through the roll; Swap, Fit, 100% and Done are along the
  top, and a double click zooms both to actual size together. The status
  line says what differs ("differ in shutter and aperture") and the note
  under the photographs which one you're editing. The one you're editing
  keeps its film and paper menus and the paper lock, as in the photo
  view. The strip makes way for them. View › Grid (G) is in the menu too.
- **Grid · Photo · Compare** in the title bar, at the right, switch
  between the views, as the View menu does.
- **Original ⌄** chooses what Original does: hold to see the whole, a
  split line, or side by side. For a RAW shot as RAW+JPEG it also chooses
  between the RAW and the camera's JPEG.
- **Your negative.** The film tray starts with "Your negative": it reads
  the photograph as a scan of a colour negative, finds the film base,
  balances and meters the print, and crops to the picture. The label says
  "Your negative read as Portra 400", and the film it's read as can be
  changed there. Prep then has Film base (Find again, Pick from rebate,
  Flat field, Crop to picture, and Backlight behind "Backlight…") and
  Balance (Auto, Pick neutral). A negative's film tray opens on Scan.
- **Flat field.** Prep › Flat field › Choose… takes a frame of the bare
  light, shot with the roll's camera, lens and aperture; each scan is
  divided by it so the light's falloff doesn't print.
- **Darkroom rolls.** Prep › Use for whole roll prints every photograph in
  view as a frame of this negative's roll: its film, backlight, base and
  filtration, each frame with its own base where it shows clear film, its
  own exposure and its crop to the picture, as one step to undo. A frame
  that sets its own filtration can go back with "Roll's filtration".
- Pick from rebate shows the whole frame, and picks land where you click
  on a straightened crop.
- **B&W colour filters** in Lab › Camera: a coloured glass in front of
  the lens for black-and-white film, with how many stops it takes.
- **Enlarger › Shifts move** links the magenta and yellow shifts:
  separately, together or opposite.
- **Flip.** The crop panel's "Flip left to right" (and View › Flip
  Horizontal) mirrors the photograph, as a negative scanned from its base
  side needs. Exports are mirrored too, and so are the tray's and the
  presets' tiles.
- **Photographs open faster.** A RAW is read once and its brightness
  match solved before anything develops, so the first develop you see is
  the right one, with no darker or lighter frame first. A photograph you
  come back to reuses its solved match instead of solving it again. As a
  photograph opens, its thumbnail stands in on the canvas at once.
- **Export the grid's selection.** With several photographs selected in
  the grid, File › Export ("Export 12 Photographs…") asks for a folder
  and exports each with its own edits and rating: the format, size and
  colour as for one, how files are named (Name_Film, Name, or your own
  pattern with {name} and {film}) and what to do when a file is there
  already (add a number, replace or skip). The status line and the
  grid's foot show how far it has come, with Cancel; Show lists what
  failed and why, with Retry Failed and Show in Explorer. An export cut
  short by quitting is offered again at the next launch.
- **Open in Lightroom.** With Lightroom or Lightroom Classic installed,
  File, the Output tab under Export and the right-click menu of the strip
  and the grid offer "Open in Lightroom": the photographs are developed
  to full-size 16-bit TIFFs and handed to Lightroom together, so it asks
  once. Copies for Lightroom are deleted once it has its own; Lightroom
  Classic's stay in Pictures › Negs for Lightroom. Settings › Upkeep ›
  Lightroom shows how much is waiting there, with a button to delete it.
  The right-click menu also gains Export.
- Negs frees the film's memory when you go to the grid, and when Windows
  runs low on memory.
